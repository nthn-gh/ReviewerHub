Early Filipino Culture

Learn about the culture, art, clothing, and architecture of our ancestors, followed by a quiz.

Study Material

1

The Concept of Culture

The culture of early Filipinos is very rich. Culture includes all the behaviors, actions, customs, traditions, values, and beliefs of a group of people as they respond to their society at a certain time.

  • Even before foreign colonizers arrived, our ancestors already had their own culture.
  • They learned this by adapting to their environment and interacting with others.
  • These cultural practices continue to be part of the different regions that make up our country at present.
2

Material and Non-Material Culture

Material Culture
Includes things that you can touch, such as homes, clothing, drinks, food, and tools.
Non-Material Culture
Includes those that are not physical in nature, such as government, education, beliefs or religion, language, and literature.
3

Art Forms and Body Decorations

The artistic skills of early Filipinos can be seen in the remains of the tools and weapons that they used. Common weapons like bows, machetes, and spears were carved with designs to make them beautiful.

  • Carvings: They carved pictures on bones, wood, ivory, or animal horns to use as decorations in their homes.
  • Tattoos: A form of body decoration that also served as a symbol of bravery in battle. The most tattooed person was hailed as the bravest. The Spaniards called early Visayans Pintados because of this.
  • Pusad or Halop: The tradition of adding gold as jewelry or decoration on their teeth.
4

Clothing of Early Filipinos

At first, clothes were made from dried animal skin and bark. They usually walked barefoot and wore jewelry like necklaces, rings, and the Kalumbiga (bracelets for women).

Men's Clothing
Kangan: sleeveless/collarless top. Bahag: lower garment. Putong: head covering.
Women's Clothing
Baro: top. Saya: skirt. Patadyong: type of skirt worn in the Visayas.
5

Architecture

Our ancestors lived a nomadic lifestyle before learning how to build houses. Their first settlements were caves or shelters made from natural materials.

Over time, as they engaged in farming, fishing, and raising animals, they learned to build structures like the bahay kubo (nipa hut), made from light materials such as cogon grass, bamboo, and palm leaves.
